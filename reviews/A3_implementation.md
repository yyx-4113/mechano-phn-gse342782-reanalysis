## Summary of the audit

The computational core of this manuscript is in unusually good shape. I recomputed **every** headline number from the two raw gzip count matrices — 24 module-contrast cells (difference, Welch *t*, Welch *P*, Mann–Whitney *P*, permutation *P*, permutation percentile), all 12 W0 expression records for both experiments, all three CCI records, all 13 gene-level contrasts, both global-shift counts and all 19 per-sample mean log2CPM values — and **not one value differs from the deposited `layer1_results.json` / `layer1_diag.json` by more than floating-point noise.** The CPM conversion, the log2(1+CPM) transform, the prefix-based group assignment, the seed-20261019 permutation machinery and the BH arithmetic are all implemented correctly, and I confirmed the permutation *P* values against exhaustive enumeration of every possible label split.

The problems are therefore not in the arithmetic but in the **provenance wrapper around it**: five of the manuscript's numbers cannot be regenerated by the deposited code, one manifest is internally stale, the root manifest is stale for three files and blind to two new ones, the deposited `.docx` silently drops all five figures, and four quantitative statements in the text are contradicted by the project's own results file. These are fixable in an afternoon, but two of them (the *q*-value provenance and the internal contradictions) bear directly on the paper's central claim of full reproducibility.

## Issues

### Issue 1 — The five *q*-values quoted in the manuscript are not produced by any deposited script

【Problem】 The BH false-discovery-rate *q*-values quoted in the abstract and Results (0.46, 0.030, 0.021, 0.016, 0.022) exist only as a hand-inserted block inside `layer1_results.json`; no script in the reproducibility package computes them, so re-running the deposited pipeline regenerates a results file **without** them.

【Evidence】 `analysis/scripts/layer1_mechano.py` builds its output dict at line 181 as `{"w0", "baseline", "panel_missing", "contrasts", "CCI"}` and writes it at lines 239–241. There is no Benjamini–Hochberg computation anywhere in `analysis/scripts/` — a search for `bh_q`/`Benjamini`/`fdr` across all four analysis scripts returns hits **only** in `manuscript/gate_consistency.py:45,49,68,71`, which *read* `R["bh_q"][...]`. Yet `analysis/results/layer1/layer1_results.json:1391–1422` contains a complete `bh_q` block for all three contrasts. Corroborating timestamps: `layer1_mechano.py` was last written 09:26:28 and `layer1_results.json` at 09:44:32 — i.e. the results file was rewritten 18 minutes after the script that is supposed to generate it, by a step that is not deposited. The arithmetic itself is right: I re-derived all 24 *q* values from the permutation *P* values with standard BH step-up and they agree to 4 decimal places (e.g. E1 under HZ: 0.029867 vs the quoted 0.030).

【Why it matters】 The manuscript makes two explicit, checkable reproducibility promises: "All numerical values quoted in this manuscript are traceable to `layer1_results.json` in the reproducibility package" (`manuscript_v1.0.0.md:61`) and "the package regenerates every number in this manuscript from the raw GEO files" (`manuscript_v1.0.0.md:116`); the cover letter repeats the second (`manuscript/cover_letter_JPR.md:16`). A reviewer or editor who downloads the package and runs it obtains a `layer1_results.json` with no `bh_q` key, and the gate then crashes with a `KeyError`. The *q*-values are the FDR-corrected evidence for the paper's only positive finding (E1, q = 0.030), so this is not a cosmetic gap. For a journal whose submission checklist includes code availability, this is the single most likely thing to be caught in editorial check.

【Specific fix】 Add the BH step to `analysis/scripts/layer1_mechano.py` immediately before the write at line 239, so the block is regenerated on every run:

```python
# ---------- BH q across the 8 pre-specified modules, within each contrast ----------
import numpy as _np
results["bh_q"] = {}
for lab, mods in results["contrasts"].items():
    keys = list(mods.keys())
    p = _np.array([mods[k]["p_perm"] for k in keys])
    m = len(p); order = _np.argsort(p); q = _np.empty(m); prev = 1.0
    for rank in range(m, 0, -1):
        i = order[rank - 1]
        prev = min(prev, p[i] * m / rank)
        q[i] = prev
    results["bh_q"][lab] = {k: float(v) for k, v in zip(keys, q)}
```

Then re-run `layer1_mechano.py`, confirm the emitted `bh_q` values are unchanged, and regenerate **both** manifests (Issue 2, Issue 3).

### Issue 4 — The manuscript contradicts itself on M4 and quotes an "all P ≥ 0.42" bound that its own primary endpoint violates

【Problem】 Two sweep statements in the text are false against the project's own results file, and one of them directly contradicts a number reported three paragraphs later.

【Evidence】 From `analysis/results/layer1/layer1_results.json` (all values reproduced by me from raw):

- Abstract (`manuscript_v1.0.0.md:19`): "the M3 stiffness programme did not shift under HZ-serum exosomes (difference −0.049 log2CPM; permutation P = 0.416; q = 0.46), nor under any other contrast (**all P ≥ 0.42**)". The primary contrast's own *P* is 0.416 < 0.42. The other two contrasts are 0.8378 (PHN) and 0.846 (VZV).
- §3.2 (`manuscript_v1.0.0.md:71`): "M3 was likewise unchanged under direct VZV infection (+0.012; P = 0.85), under PHN exosomes (−0.017; P = 0.84), and for M4 mechano-effectors in every contrast (**all P ≥ 0.11**)". Actual M4 permutation *P* values: 0.4598 (HZ), **0.1084** (PHN), **0.0242** (VZV).
- §3.4 (`manuscript_v1.0.0.md:79`) then reports the VZV result as "M4 mechano-effectors were suppressed (-0.054; P = 0.024)" — i.e. the same manuscript asserts both "all P ≥ 0.11" and "P = 0.024".
- Neither sweep is covered by the consistency gate (see Issue 9), which is why both survived 74 passing assertions.

【Why it matters】 These are the sentences a statistically literate editor reads first when assessing whether a null result is being oversold. An internal contradiction between §3.2 and §3.4 on the same module, in the same submission, reads as careless reporting of a negative result and invites a request for major revision on the primary endpoint's framing — even though the underlying numbers are correct.

【Specific fix】 Replace the abstract sentence with:

> "The pre-registered primary endpoint was a correctly-powered null: the M3 stiffness programme did not shift under HZ-serum exosomes (difference −0.049 log2CPM; permutation P = 0.416; q = 0.46), nor under PHN exosomes (−0.017; P = 0.84) or direct VZV infection (+0.012; P = 0.85)."

and replace the §3.2 clause with:

> "The null was not contrast-specific: M3 was likewise unchanged under direct VZV infection (+0.012; P = 0.85) and under PHN exosomes (−0.017; P = 0.84). Among the remaining mechano-modules, M4 was unchanged under HZ exosomes (P = 0.46) and PHN exosomes (P = 0.11) but was suppressed under direct VZV infection (−0.054; P = 0.024), and M2 showed a borderline shift under HZ exosomes (−0.148; P = 0.068)."

Then add two gate assertions (see Issue 9) so the sweeps cannot drift again.

### Issue 5 — "M1 is effectively a PIEZO2-dominated module" is not supported: P2RX3 carries three-quarters of the module's expression mass

【Problem】 The data-examination conclusion that M1 is "PIEZO2-driven" is wrong on the expression scale the module score actually uses; within M1, P2RX3 is the dominant transcript by a wide margin.

【Evidence】 Mean CPM over the 12 DirectInf samples, recomputed from `GSE342782_Processed_DirectInf_neuronHD10s_BulkSeq_Counts.csv.gz`: P2RX3 **806.84**, PIEZO2 **245.24**, TMEM150C 14.41, PIEZO1 2.64, KCNK4 1.49, TRPV4 0.251, KCNK2 0.068, TRPA1 0.062, STOML3 0.0077. P2RX3 is 75.3% of the module's total CPM and 3.3× PIEZO2. In log2(1+CPM) space, which is what the module score averages, the ordering is the same and starker: P2RX3 9.651 vs PIEZO2 7.940. The SeraExo experiment agrees (P2RX3 470.6 CPM, log2CPM 8.853 vs PIEZO2 164.1 CPM, log2CPM 7.324). The claim appears in the abstract (`manuscript_v1.0.0.md:19`: "qualifying M1 as PIEZO2-driven"), §3.1 (`manuscript_v1.0.0.md:67`: "M1 is effectively a PIEZO2-dominated module") and in Figure 1 (`analysis/scripts/fig_extra.py:33`: "M1 = PIEZO2-driven").

【Why it matters】 The "PIEZO2-spared / electrochemical-suppressed" dissociation is the paper's mechanistic story, and it rests on M1 behaving as a PIEZO2 proxy. In this dataset M1 behaves largely as a P2RX3 proxy, and P2RX3 is an ATP-gated purinergic receptor rather than a mechanogated channel — so its presence in a module labelled "mechanogated channels" is itself contestable. A reviewer who opens the count matrix will find this in five minutes, and it weakens the "channel-imbalance" interpretation precisely where it is strongest.

【Specific fix】 Replace the two sentences with:

> "PIEZO2 was highly expressed (mean CPM 245.2 in the infection experiment; detected in 100% of samples in both experiments), as were ITGB1 (323.6 CPM) and the electrochemical nociceptor channels SCN9A (log2CPM 8.31) and TRPV1 (6.94). In contrast, TRPV4 (0.25 CPM), STOML3 (0.01 CPM), LOX (0.41 CPM) and MMP9 (0.11 CPM) fell below the detection threshold. Within M1, PIEZO2 is the only robustly expressed mechanogated channel; the module's expression mass is nonetheless dominated by P2RX3 (mean CPM 806.8, log2CPM 9.65), an ATP-gated rather than mechanogated receptor, and M1 should therefore be read as a PIEZO2-plus-P2RX3 composite rather than a PIEZO2 proxy."

and update `fig_extra.py:33` accordingly.

### Issue 6 — "All other modules were fully represented" is false

【Problem】 The Methods state that only M3 lost genes to symbol absence; in fact two neutral-control modules also lost genes, and the manuscript never mentions it.

【Evidence】 `manuscript_v1.0.0.md:53`: "M3 used 56 of 58 genes: CYR61 and CTGF were absent from the count matrix; **all other modules were fully represented**." But `analysis/results/layer1/layer1_results.json` records `panel_missing.N2_olfactory_control = [OR6A1, OR10A1, OR13C1]` and `panel_missing.N3_ribosomal_control = [RPS4]`, and the per-contrast records show `genes_used` 13 of 16 for N2 and 17 of 18 for N3 (I confirmed all four symbols are genuinely absent from both matrices).

【Why it matters】 The neutral controls are the paper's calibration device — the argument that the PHN contrast is "non-specific" depends on N1/N2/N3 being intact, pre-specified comparators. Silently dropping 3 of 16 olfactory receptors and 1 of 18 ribosomal proteins, while telling the reader every other module was complete, is exactly the kind of selective disclosure that erodes trust in a pre-registration claim. It also matters numerically: N2's score is computed on 13 genes of which 7 are identically zero in both groups, which is why a "significant" −0.022 shift is being read as a noise-floor artefact.

【Specific fix】 Replace the sentence with:

> "Module scores used every panel gene present in the matrix: M3 used 56 of 58 genes (CYR61 and CTGF absent), N2 used 13 of 16 (OR6A1, OR10A1, OR13C1 absent) and N3 used 17 of 18 (RPS4 absent); all other modules were fully represented. Detection was defined as CPM > 1."

### Issue 7 — "CYR61 and CTGF were absent from the count matrix" is a symbol-alias artefact; both genes are in the matrix under their current HGNC symbols

【Problem】 The two genes reported as missing from the matrix are present in it; only the frozen panel's legacy symbols are absent.

【Evidence】 I searched both raw matrices: `CYR61` and `CTGF` are absent, but **`CCN1` and `CCN2` are present in both** (`CCN1`/`CCN2` are the current HGNC-approved symbols for CYR61/CTGF). The claim is made at `manuscript_v1.0.0.md:53` and repeated as a limitation at `manuscript_v1.0.0.md:105` ("Two panel genes (CYR61, CTGF) were absent from the processed matrix"). The same alias problem affects N3's "missing" RPS4, which is present as `RPS4X`. Sensitivity analysis I ran from raw: scoring M3 with CCN1 and CCN2 substituted for CYR61 and CTGF (58/58 genes) moves the primary endpoint from −0.048878 to **−0.047844** (permutation *P* 0.416 → 0.4098; exact-enumeration *P* 0.4219 → 0.4220) and the PHN M3 from −0.016741 to −0.019263 (*P* 0.8378 → 0.8068). The conclusions are unchanged.

【Why it matters】 A limitation that is really a stale-symbol problem reads as a data problem, and it slightly misstates the evidence base for the "inert stiffness programme" claim. It is also the kind of thing a careful reviewer checks, because "two genes missing from a GEO processed matrix" is an unusual claim.

【Specific fix】 Update the panel and the text:

> "Module scores used every panel gene present in the matrix. CYR61 and CTGF are present in the matrix under their current HGNC symbols CCN1 and CCN2 and were mapped accordingly (M3 therefore used 58 of 58 genes); N3's RPS4 is present as RPS4X. Detection was defined as CPM > 1."

Concretely: add an alias map (`{"CYR61": "CCN1", "CTGF": "CCN2", "RPS4": "RPS4X"}`) to `layer1_mechano.py`'s gene lookup in `module_score()` (lines 64–69), re-run, and report the 58-gene M3 sensitivity result in §3.2 so readers can see the primary endpoint is robust to the mapping.

### Issue 9 — The gate verifies 74 assertions but leaves four manuscript numbers unguarded, and one assertion is hardcoded against its own stated rule

【Problem】 The consistency gate is genuine and mostly sound, but it does not cover several numbers the manuscript states, and one assertion's expected value is hardcoded, contradicting the script's own discipline statement.

【Evidence】 I read `manuscript/gate_consistency.py` in full and the 74-assertion report (`analysis/results/layer1/gate_consistency_report.txt`: "74 assertions, 0 failures"). The assertion count is accurate (34 module-level + 1 CCI bound + 9 W0 + 4 global + 26 gene-level = 74). I hand-verified five assertions against the results JSON and my own recomputation:

| Assertion | Fragment | JSON expected | My recomputation | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `primary M3 diff` (line 43) | "difference -0.049 log2CPM" | −0.04887804878732682 | −0.04887804878732682 | PASS, correct |
| `E1 q` (line 49) | "q = 0.030" | 0.0299 | 0.0298667 (BH step-up) | PASS, correct |
| `VZV M1 welch p` (line 60) | "P = 0.047" | 0.04655764245345769 | 0.04655764245345769 | PASS, correct |
| `HZ up genes` (line 99) | "129/174 under HZ exosomes" | 129 | 129 | PASS, correct |
| `global range high` (line 98) | "1.61" | 1.605 | 1.605447 | PASS, but only because the tolerance (0.005) exactly equals the gap; 1.605447 rounds legitimately to 1.61, so the manuscript is right and the gate is merely at its limit |

The weaknesses:

1. **Hardcoded expectation.** `manuscript/gate_consistency.py:93` reads `numchk("PIEZO2 spared p", "P = 0.18", None if False else mod(...)["p_perm"] if False else 0.1816)` — a dead-code expression whose only live branch is the literal `0.1816`. This directly violates the script's own docstring, "禁止硬编码期望" ("no hardcoded expectations", line 5). The value happens to be right (I recompute PIEZO2's Welch *P* as 0.18155288126233107), but the assertion is not derived from data and would not catch a change in the pipeline.
2. **Uncovered numbers.** The gate contains no assertion for: the abstract's "all P ≥ 0.42" (Issue 4); §3.2's "all P ≥ 0.11" (Issue 4); the PHN 568/125 counts (Issue 8); N2's q = 0.022 (`manuscript_v1.0.0.md:87`); the VZV and PHN M3 differences +0.012 / −0.017 (`manuscript_v1.0.0.md:71`); the 100% detection fractions; "56 of 58 genes"; the file sizes 622,501 / 884,411 bytes (`manuscript_v1.0.0.md:41`); "31 samples"; or the module gene counts in §2.2.
3. **Position-blind fragment matching.** `numchk` (lines 20–29) checks that a fragment occurs *somewhere* in the manuscript and that *some* number inside it matches. For the gene-level loop (lines 138–148) the fragment is the bare *P*-string, so `"0.18"` for YAP1 is satisfied by PIEZO2's "P = 0.18" elsewhere in the text; the assertion cannot detect a swapped or mis-attributed gene-level *P* value.
4. **Not automated.** `.github/workflows/release.yml` only zips the package (lines 20–24); the gate is never run in CI, so nothing prevents the manuscript and the results file from drifting apart after the last manual run.

【Why it matters】 The gate is the mechanism the authors offer in place of reviewer trust ("All numerical values quoted in this manuscript are traceable to `layer1_results.json`"). Its two blind spots — hardcoded expectations and uncovered sweeps — are exactly where the two false statements in Issue 4 live, so the gate currently certifies a text that contradicts itself. That is a credibility problem disproportionate to the effort of fixing it.

【Specific fix】 (a) Replace line 93 with a data-derived expectation, e.g. `numchk("PIEZO2 spared p", "(-0.282, P = 0.18)", gene_val("PIEZO2")[1])` using the `gene_val` helper already defined at lines 123–127. (b) Add assertions for every number listed in point 2, in particular two `claim_le`-style sweep checks:

```python
m3ps = [mod(l, "M3_ecm_integrin_hippo")["p_perm"] for l in C]
claim_le("M3 sweep claim", "all P >= 0.42", min(m3ps))          # manuscript must state the true minimum
m4ps = [mod(l, "M4_mechano_effectors")["p_perm"] for l in C]
claim_le("M4 sweep claim", "all P >= 0.11", min(m4ps))
```

(c) Tighten `numchk` for gene-level rows to match the full `"GENE (LFC, P = p)"` fragment rather than the bare *P* string, and (d) add a `gate_consistency.py` step to `release.yml` so it runs on every tagged build.

### Issue 10 — The deposited `.docx` contains none of the five figures and does not match its own build script

【Problem】 The Word version of the manuscript silently drops all five figures and all five figure captions, and cannot be reproduced by the deposited `build_docx.py`.

【Evidence】 `manuscript/manuscript_v1.0.0.docx` has **zero** images: `word/document.xml` contains 0 `<w:drawing>` elements, 0 `a:blip` references and 0 `<w:pict>` elements, and the archive contains no `word/media/` entry. Yet `manuscript/build_docx.py:65–79` inserts `fig1_design.png` after the "3.1 Data examination" heading and `fig2a`–`fig2d` after the "3.4 Double dissociation" heading, each followed by a caption. The deposited docx contains no "Figure" string at all. By contrast the **text** conversion is faithful: all 51 body lines of the `.md` appear in the docx, every numeric token in the `.md` appears in the docx and vice versa (no numeric drift in either direction), all 14 references and the reference-verification note are present, and headings, bullets and emphasis survive. File timestamps: `build_docx.py` 09:59:00, `.docx` 11:29:06 — the docx post-dates the script but was evidently produced by something else.

### Issue 14 — Three of the 24 permutation *P* values are anti-conservative relative to exact enumeration, because B = 5,000 is coarser than the text implies

【Problem】 The label-permutation machinery is implemented correctly, but at these sample sizes the permutation space is small enough to enumerate exactly, and the B = 5,000 estimates that the manuscript quotes as three- or four-significant-figure numbers are off by factors of 2–6 in the small-*P* cells.

【Evidence】 I enumerated **every** possible label split for each contrast — C(14,7) = 3,432 for HZ-exo vs control-exo, C(12,7) = 792 for PHN-exo vs control-exo, C(12,6) = 924 for VZV vs mock — and compared the exact two-sided *P* with the pipeline's B = 5,000 estimate (seed 20261019). 21 of 24 module-contrast cells agree within Monte-Carlo error. Three do not:

| Cell | Manuscript quotes | Exact enumeration | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| HZ N3 (ribosomal) | P = 0.0006 | 0.00175 (6/3432) | 2.9× anti-conservative |
| PHN N3 (ribosomal) | P = 0.0002 | 0.00126 (1/792) | 6.3× anti-conservative |
| VZV M4 (mechano-effectors) | P = 0.0242 | 0.03030 | 1.25× anti-conservative |

The primary and secondary endpoints are unaffected in direction or significance: M3 exact 0.42191 (quoted 0.416), E1 exact 0.01282 (quoted 0.011), and the BH *q* for E1 computed from the exact *P* is 0.034 rather than 0.030 — still < 0.05. The floor of the estimator is 1/5000 = 0.0002 (`layer1_mechano.py:83`), which is why "P = 0.0002" and "P = 0.0006" are single-draw counts presented as if they were precise.

【Why it matters】 The N3 results are quoted in §3.6 as evidence of a "genuine translational-programme response"; quoting P = 0.0002 for a quantity whose exact value is 0.00126 overstates the evidence in the *P* metric. It also creates an avoidable inconsistency: the same results file reports M4 under direct infection as P = 0.024 while the exact value is 0.030, i.e. the "borderline" language in §3.4 is if anything generous.

【Specific fix】 Because the permutation spaces are exhaustively enumerable at these sample sizes, add an exact-enumeration function alongside `perm_null` and report the exact *P* (spec: enumerate all C(n, n_A) splits of the contrast's own sample indices, compute the difference of module-score means for each, and take the two-sided proportion with |d| ≥ |d_obs|):

```python
def perm_exact(diff_obs, mat, idx_a, idx_b):
    pool = list(idx_a) + list(idx_b); na = len(idx_a); ng = mat.shape[0]
    cs = mat.sum(axis=0); tot = 0; cnt = 0
    for comb in itertools.combinations(pool, na):
        sel = set(comb); rest = set(pool) - sel
        d = (cs[list(rest)].sum() / (len(pool) - na) - cs[list(sel)].sum() / na) / ng
        tot += 1; cnt += abs(d) >= abs(diff_obs) - 1e-12
    return cnt / tot
```

Report exact *P* in the JSON/CSV and quote it in the text for the three affected cells; alternatively raise B to 200,000 and report the Monte-Carlo standard error.

## Overall verdict

**Major revision — the numbers are right, the package is not (yet).**

This is a rare case where the hardest part is already done correctly: I could not find a single arithmetic error in the manuscript. Every module score, every permutation *P* value, every *q* value, every gene-level contrast, every detection fraction and every global-shift count reproduces exactly from the raw GEO files, the group assignment is corroborated sample-by-sample against the series metadata, and the permutation machinery is a valid, deterministic label-permutation null. The pre-registration apparatus (frozen panel tag, fixed seed, B = 5,000) is real and checkable.

What fails is the reproducibility wrapper that the manuscript explicitly promises, plus four statements in the text that contradict the project's own results file. Five numbers (the *q*-values) cannot be regenerated by the deposited code; the results manifest is stale; the root manifest is wrong for three files and blind to two; the Word file silently drops all five figures; and two "sweep" claims in the abstract and §3.2 are false, one of them contradicting §3.4 in the same manuscript. None of these changes the scientific conclusion — the primary null, the E1 secondary signal and the route-dependent dissociation all survive my recomputation, including a 58-gene M3 sensitivity analysis and exact-enumeration *P* values. But as submitted, a reader who follows the Data availability instructions will hit a `KeyError` and a broken checksum within minutes, and an editor who reads the abstract and §3.2 together will find a self-contradiction. Both are fixable in an afternoon of code and copy-editing, and both must be fixed before this can go to external review.

I recommend major revision with a specific, bounded list of provenance and text corrections (items 1–13 above); no new analysis is required beyond the N1 gene-disjointness re-run and the power/MDE statement.
