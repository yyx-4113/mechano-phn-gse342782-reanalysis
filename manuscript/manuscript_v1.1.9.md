## Abstract

**Background.** Mechanical allodynia is a common, clinically prominent feature of postherpetic neuralgia (PHN), yet mechanotransduction is rarely examined in PHN transcriptomics. GSE342782 (RNA-sequencing of an immortalized human sensory-neuron line (HD10.6) exposed to varicella-zoster virus (VZV) or serum exosomes from controls, acute herpes zoster (HZ) and PHN patients) permits a test.

**Methods.** We froze a four-module mechanotransduction panel (M1-M4), an electrochemical nociceptor module (E1) and three neutral controls. The single primary endpoint was the M3 (ECM-integrin-Hippo) difference between HZ and control exosomes. Inference used Welch t and exact label-permutation nulls with Benjamini-Hochberg correction.

**Results.** M3 did not shift under HZ-serum exosomes (-0.049 log2CPM; P = 0.42; minimum detectable effect 0.18 log2CPM), a bounded null. HZ-serum exosomes suppressed the nociceptor module (E1, -0.220 log2CPM; P = 0.013; q = 0.034), led by TRPV1, the only gene surviving within-E1 correction (q = 0.017); KCNS1, SCN9A, ASIC1 and ASIC2 were only nominally down. M1 was resolution-limited (P = 0.11; detected-gene subset P = 0.046); E1 was not separable from neutral control N1 (E1 - N1 = -0.064, P = 0.18). A route-by-program difference-in-differences supports route dependence (-0.193; P = 0.0011); a post hoc E1-M1 interaction (-0.142; P = 0.0017) shrank among detected genes (-0.072; P = 0.16). The exploratory PHN-exosome contrast was non-specific and donor-confounded.

**Conclusions.** In this 36-h exposure of an immortalized embryonic dorsal-root-ganglion line, HZ-serum exosomes suppressed the electrogenic nociceptor module, whereas direct VZV infection produced no module-level E1 shift and a lesser TRPV1 decrease; the ECM-Hippo stiffness program showed no detectable response within a bounded null. Relevance to PHN allodynia is hypothesis-generating, not evidence of mechanism in patients.

**Registration.** Protocol v0.4 and frozen panel `mtp-frozen-2026-10-19` were committed to the public repository before data examination; no external registry entry.

**Keywords:** postherpetic neuralgia; mechanical allodynia; mechanotransduction; PIEZO2; varicella-zoster virus; extracellular vesicles

## 1. Introduction

Postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) is defined as dermatomal pain persisting at least 90 days after the onset of the acute herpes zoster rash, and is clinically characterized in many patients by mechanical allodynia (innocuous cutaneous stimuli transduced as pain), which motivates the focus on mechanotransduction in this reanalysis.^1,2^ Mendelian-randomization analyses have begun to map the causal determinants of PHN risk.^3^ The symptom is, at its core, a disturbance of mechanotransduction, yet the genomic literature of PHN has seldom examined it through that lens. Published bioinformatic reanalyses of PHN cohorts have concentrated on interferon, immune, and inflammatory programs;^4^ to our knowledge, no published reanalysis has systematically examined the mechanotransduction machinery (mechanogated ion channels, the membrane-cytoskeleton interface, or the extracellular-matrix (ECM)-integrin-Hippo axis) in PHN transcriptomic data.

Two developments make such an examination timely. First, varicella-zoster virus (VZV) establishes latency in dorsal root and cranial nerve ganglia and reactivates to cause herpes zoster and PHN.^5^ Niemeyer and colleagues released GSE342782, a bulk RNA-sequencing resource in which differentiated HD10.6 human sensory neurons were exposed to direct VZV infection (harvested 4 days post-infection) or to serum-derived exosomes from healthy controls, patients with acute HZ, or patients with PHN.^6^ The original study reported down-regulation of canonical nociceptive ion channels, induction of substance-P-related neuropeptides, and ECM-remodeling signals, but did not examine these findings through a mechanotransduction lens. Second, extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT), the deliberate application of controlled mechanical stress, has shown within-group benefit in small uncontrolled or single-blind studies^7,8^ and, in the largest randomized trial (n = 100), lower between-group numerical-rating-scale scores without a significant time-by-group interaction^9^ (protocol for that trial:^10^); its proposed mechanisms involve integrin-FAK-p38MAPK and Piezo1–FAK signaling in non-neuronal model systems,^11,12^ and none of these studies tested a YAP/TAZ or neuronal-channel mechanism in PHN, so any link to the present neuronal transcriptome is speculative. The mechanical axis is invoked at both the disease end (allodynia) and the treatment end (mechanotherapy) of PHN, yet no study has connected them transcriptomically, and the present data do not.

Here we report a prospectively specified reanalysis of GSE342782 with a frozen mechanotransduction panel, a single pre-specified primary endpoint, neutral-control calibration, and full permutation-based inference. The analysis was designed to answer three questions. (i) Are the mechanotransduction modules detectable, and do they shift, in human sensory neurons exposed to VZV or patient exosomes? (ii) Does the ECM-Hippo "stiffness program" (the hypothesized core of the mechanical axis) respond to any perturbation route? (iii) Do direct viral infection and exosome-mediated signaling converge on, or dissociate between, mechanogated versus electrochemical channel programs?

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Data source and design

GSE342782 (GEO accession GSE342782; BioProject PRJNA1508885) comprises two experiments in differentiated HD10.6 human sensory neurons.^6^ Experiment 1 ("DirectInf"): mock infection (n = 6) versus VZV infection (n = 6), harvested 4 days post-infection. Experiment 2 ("SeraExo"): 36-hour exposure to serum-derived exosomes from healthy controls (n = 7), patients with acute HZ (n = 7), or patients with PHN (n = 5). Processed RSEM gene-level counts were downloaded directly from the GEO supplementary files (DirectInf counts, 622,501 bytes; SeraExo counts, 884,411 bytes; gzip integrity verified).^13^ Sample-to-group assignment used the column prefixes of the count matrices (C = control, P = PHN, S = acute zoster), which match the group sizes reported in the series metadata (7/5/7). Because this is a secondary analysis of publicly deposited data, reporting follows the items of STROBE applicable to analyses of existing datasets; items that depend on information absent from the public metadata (donor age, sex, and collection dates) are explicitly identified as unreportable.

### 2.2 Prospectively specified analysis plan

Before any expression data were examined, the following were fixed in writing and deposited in a versioned public protocol (protocol v0.4; panel tag `mtp-frozen-2026-10-19`):

- **Frozen panel.** Four mechanotransduction modules, M1 mechanogated channels (PIEZO1, PIEZO2, TRPV4, STOML3, KCNK2/4, TMEM150C, P2RX3, TRPA1; TRPA1 is included for completeness, as its direct mechanical gating is debated in the current literature and it is effectively undetected in this dataset), M2 membrane-cytoskeleton coupling (20 genes), M3 ECM-integrin-Hippo (58 genes) and M4 mechano-effectors (25 genes), plus E1, an electrochemical nociceptor module (SCN9A, SCN10A, SCN11A, TRPV1, TRPM8, ASIC1/2/3, and related genes, excluding M1 members), and three neutral controls: N1 voltage-gated channels, N2 olfactory receptors, N3 ribosomal proteins. The non-canonical E1 members (GRIA1, GRIA2, GRIN2B, P2RX7, CACNA1H) were retained because excitatory ionotropic glutamate and purinergic receptors are reported in human DRG neurons;^14^ a canonical-channel sensitivity analysis is reported in §3.3. Module coverage after matching against the count matrix: M1, M2, M4, E1 and N1 were fully represented (9/9, 20/20, 25/25, 18/18 and 17/17 genes respectively); M3 was represented by 56 of 58 genes (CYR61 and CTGF absent; the CCN1/CCN2 orthologs were present and used in an alias sensitivity analysis); N2 by 13 of 16 (OR6A1, OR10A1, OR13C1 absent); N3 by 17 of 18 (RPS4 absent; an RPS4X ortholog was present). PTK2 (FAK) appears in both M3 and M4 in the frozen panel; sensitivities excluding it are reported in §3.2.
- **Primary endpoint.** The M3 module-score difference between HZ-serum exosomes and control exosomes (direction, effect size, permutation quantile). All other tests were pre-classified as secondary (E1 and M1 under the same contrast; the "channel-imbalance" hypothesis) or exploratory (PHN-exosome contrast; direct-infection contrast).
- **Donor-confounding safeguard.** Because the original authors reported that the PHN exosome donors were older and almost exclusively female,^6^ the PHN contrast was pre-designated exploratory and the HZ-versus-control contrast was designated the primary comparison.

### 2.3 Preprocessing and module scoring

Counts were converted to counts-per-million (CPM) and module scores computed as the mean log2(1 + CPM) across panel genes present in the matrix. Detection was defined as CPM > 1. Throughout this paper the shorthand "log2CPM" denotes log2(1 + CPM); the unit offset avoids undefined values for zero-count genes and is applied identically to every gene and sample. Because the exosome response included a ribosomal-component shift (138 RPL/RPS genes rose from 3.23% to 4.22% of the library), which could in principle dilute non-ribosomal transcripts artifactually, the primary contrast was repeated under a DESeq2-style median-of-ratios sensitivity analysis (specified post hoc): size factors were computed as the median of CPM/gene-wise-geometric-mean ratios across the 17,922 genes with non-zero counts in all 19 exosome samples and applied on the CPM scale as log2(1 + CPM/size factor); every headline conclusion was unchanged (E1 -0.194, permutation P = 0.015; M3 -0.024, P = 0.64; E1 - M1 interaction P = 0.0017), so the E1 suppression is not a normalization artifact (`layer1_norm_robustness.json`).

### 2.4 Statistical testing

Group differences in module scores were tested with Welch's *t*-test, and calibrated against an exact label-permutation null that enumerates every C(n1+n2, n1) partition of the observed per-sample module scores (no Monte-Carlo approximation, no 1/B floor), reporting a two-sided empirical P value and the observed difference's percentile within the null. Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction was applied across the eight modules within each contrast.^15^ The channel-complementarity index (CCI), the within-sample difference between the mean expression rank of M1 genes and that of E1 genes across all 43,236 matrix genes, was computed per sample and compared between groups with the same permutation scheme. Gene-level contrasts were evaluated as Welch *t*-tests on log2(1 + CPM) values and, for genes belonging to a panel module, were further subjected to Benjamini–Hochberg correction within that module (reported as q_within_module). Both the uncorrected P values and the within-module q values are presented as descriptive support for the module-level findings, not as separately corrected discovery claims. The minimum detectable effect at 80% power (two-sided α = 0.05) was computed from the pooled within-group standard deviation of the per-sample module scores under an equal-variance normal approximation with a non-central t distribution. Confidence intervals for module-score differences are reported both as Welch (Satterthwaite) intervals and as permutation-inversion intervals, [D − q0.975, D − q0.025] of the exact permutation null. Mann-Whitney U P values are deposited in the reproducibility package and are not relied upon for any claim.

### 2.5 Reproducibility

All analyses were executed in Python 3.14.5 (NumPy 2.5.1, pandas 3.0.5, SciPy 1.18.0, Matplotlib 3.11.1). Every result file carries a SHA-256 manifest linking it to the exact input files and the frozen panel; the results-directory `MANIFEST.json` covers every deposited Layer-1 result file, including the normalization-robustness and extra-checks artifacts. All numerical values quoted in this manuscript are traceable to the reproducibility package: the module- and contrast-level results in `layer1_results.json`, the gene-level results in `layer1_gene_level.csv` (which includes a within-module Benjamini–Hochberg q), and the post hoc and robustness analyses in `layer1_extra_checks.json`, `layer1_norm_robustness.json` and `layer1_round2_additions.json`, all under the SHA-256 MANIFEST.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Data examination: PIEZO2 and P2RX3 are highly expressed; TRPV4 and STOML3 are not detectable

The prospectively specified data-examination gate established the expression baseline of the model (Table 1); the design and analysis pipeline are summarized in Figure 1. The HD10.6 line, a tetracycline-regulatable v-myc immortalized embryonic dorsal root ganglion clonal line, not an iPSC-derived model^16,17^, expressed PIEZO2 at high levels (mean 245.2 CPM; mean log2CPM 7.94 across all 12 infection-experiment samples; detected in 100% of samples in both experiments), as were ITGB1 (323.6 CPM) and the electrochemical nociceptor channels SCN9A (mean log2CPM 8.31 across all 12 samples; 8.39 in mock samples only) and TRPV1 (6.94 across all 12 samples; 7.02 in mock samples only). In contrast, TRPV4 (0.25 CPM), STOML3 (0.01 CPM), LOX (0.41 CPM) and MMP9 (0.11 CPM) fell below the detection threshold. Within M1, P2RX3 was the most abundant transcript (806.8 CPM in the infection experiment; 470.6 CPM in the exosome experiment), exceeding PIEZO2 (245.2 / 164.1 CPM); four of nine M1 genes sat at or below the detection floor. Two consequences follow. First, the technical risk that the model fails to express PIEZO2 is refuted, but M1 is more precisely a P2RX3/PIEZO2-dominated module than a PIEZO2-dominated one. Second, inference about TRPV4-, STOML3- or MMP9-centered mechanisms is out of scope for this dataset.

### 3.2 Primary endpoint: the ECM-Hippo stiffness program is transcriptionally inert within a bounded null

The pre-specified primary endpoint, the M3 difference between HZ-serum exosomes and control exosomes, was -0.049 log2CPM (permutation P = 0.42; BH q = 0.46). We therefore report a bounded null: with seven versus seven samples and a 56-gene module, the minimum detectable effect at 80% power, computed from the observed group SDs as a post hoc sensitivity, was 0.18 log2CPM, and the observed difference sits well inside its 95% confidence interval (Welch -0.18 to +0.08; permutation-inversion -0.16 to +0.06). The null was not contrast-specific: M3 was likewise unchanged under direct VZV infection (P = 0.85), under PHN exosomes (P = 0.83), and when the M3 score was recomputed with the CCN1/CCN2 orthologs included (58 genes: -0.048; P = 0.42). M4 mechano-effectors was unchanged under HZ exosomes (-0.028; permutation P = 0.46) and modestly suppressed under direct VZV infection (-0.054; permutation P = 0.03; BH q = 0.20 across the eight modules), a borderline nominal signal; the route difference for M4 was not itself significant (+0.027; exact permutation P = 0.58), so no route specificity is claimed for M4. At the gene level the M3 module was directionally incoherent: COL4A1 (-0.381, P = 0.005), COL4A2 (-0.627, P = 0.007), ADAMTS1 (-0.479, P = 0.013) and TEAD1 (-0.339, P = 0.047) moved downward while LOX (+0.291, P = 0.024) and YAP1 (+0.425, P = 0.18) moved upward (these are uncorrected nominal P values across 56 genes, shown only to illustrate directional incoherence); LOX, mean CPM 0.40, and YAP1, 1.04, are themselves at or near the detection floor and are listed for completeness only, and the directional incoherence among detected M3 genes (COL4A1, COL4A2, ADAMTS1, TEAD1 and LOXL2 down versus MOB1A, TEAD2 and PLOD2 up) is unchanged when they are excluded. Excluding PTK2 (which the frozen panel places in both M3 and M4) changed neither module (M3 minus PTK2: -0.047 log2CPM, P = 0.44; M4 minus PTK2: -0.024, P = 0.52). This null is a substantive result: at the neuronal mRNA level, and within 36 hours of exosome exposure, the stiffness program does not respond to VZV-related perturbation.

### 3.3 Secondary endpoint: HZ-serum exosomes suppress electrochemical nociceptor channels

Under the same primary contrast, the pre-specified secondary module E1 (electrochemical nociceptor channels) was significantly suppressed (-0.220 log2CPM; exact permutation P = 0.013; Welch P = 0.011; BH q = 0.034; 95% CI -0.38 to -0.06; Figure 2a), whereas all four mechano-modules were not at the full-module level (M1 -0.078, P = 0.11; M2 -0.148, P = 0.068). Gene-level analysis showed a downward shift across the classical nociceptor apparatus, but the support is weaker than a module-level claim once within-module multiplicity is considered. In an uncorrected, descriptive screen (Welch *t*; E1 n = 18 genes), TRPV1 was down (-0.567, P = 0.0009) and remained significant after Benjamini–Hochberg correction within the E1 module (q = 0.017; Figure 2b); KCNS1 (-0.393, P = 0.017), SCN9A (-0.323, P = 0.018), ASIC1 (-0.181, P = 0.019) and ASIC2 (-0.296, P = 0.042) were nominally significant but did not survive within-E1 correction (q ≈ 0.07–0.13). By contrast, no mechanogated M1 gene reached P < 0.05 (PIEZO2 P = 0.18, PIEZO1 P = 0.10, P2RX3 P = 0.076). SCN10A (Nav1.8) is excluded from the confirmed list: its mean CPM in the exosome experiment was 0.55 (detected in only 11% of samples), below the study's own detection threshold, although its nominal down-regulation (log2FC -0.384, P = 0.008, the second-smallest P value within E1 after TRPV1; within-E1 q = 0.068) is reported as a floor-limited descriptive value, not a confirmed signal. Within the membrane-cytoskeleton module M2, two genes also survived within-module correction (EZR, -0.335, Welch P = 0.0008, q = 0.017; PXN, -0.210, P = 0.002, q = 0.019); because the M2 module as a whole was not shifted (P = 0.068), these are reported as descriptive gene-level observations and are not claimed as module-level effects. A post hoc robustness analysis restricting E1 to 13 canonical nociceptor channels (SCN9A, SCN10A, SCN11A, TRPV1, TRPM8, ASIC1/2/3, KCNS1, KCNQ2/3, CACNA2D1/2) gave a larger shift than the full module (-0.244 log2CPM; Welch P = 0.0037; exact permutation P = 0.0023; 95% CI -0.39 to -0.10; detected genes only, n = 10: -0.257, P = 0.0029), so the E1 finding does not depend on its non-canonical members. PIEZO2, the dominant mechanogated channel of this model, was not suppressed (-0.282, P = 0.18; 95% CI approximately -0.72 to +0.15), a resolution-limited null at the gene level. We therefore report the channel-class imbalance as a gene-level, nominal signal led by TRPV1 rather than a confirmed module-level selective suppression; the M1 pattern is itself resolution-limited, because restricting to detected genes flips M1 to significant (P = 0.046, §3.6), so neither "spared" nor "confirmed down" is established for M1. Under the same eight-module correction the two neutral controls also crossed q < 0.05 (N3 q = 0.014; N2 q = 0.023; §3.6). E1 must also be judged against its own neutral control: the per-sample (E1 - N1) difference shifted by only -0.064 log2CPM under HZ exosomes (exact permutation P = 0.178; Welch P = 0.187), ie E1 is not separable from N1 at the module level. The channel-class imbalance therefore survives as a gene-level, family-corrected signal led by TRPV1, not as a demonstrated module-level selectivity over control channels.

### 3.4 Route dependence tested by contrast-of-contrasts, not by significant-versus-not

To test whether the route determines which channel program shifts, we computed the interaction (difference-in-differences) between the E1 and M1 module scores. For the exosome contrast, (E1 difference - M1 difference) = -0.142 log2CPM with exact permutation P = 0.0017 (Figure 2c); for the direct-infection contrast, (M1 difference - E1 difference) = -0.051 with exact permutation P = 0.19, so the reciprocal "M1-selective" pattern is not supported at the interaction level. Testing whether the route modifies the E1-versus-M1 pattern directly, the difference between the exosome and direct-infection difference-in-differences (E1−M1 composite, same orientation in both routes) was -0.193 log2CPM, exact permutation P = 0.0011 (3,356 of 3,171,168 combined label partitions); this formal route-by-program interaction supports route dependence, while noting that SCN10A contributes appreciably to the E1 score only in the infection experiment, so the composite is not identically composed across routes. However, the exosome interaction does not survive a sensitivity analysis for detection-floor asymmetry between modules: M1 contains 4/9 genes below the detection threshold (each contributing ≈0 and diluting its module mean), whereas E1 contains 5/18. Restricting both modules to detected genes (mean CPM > 1) removes this dilution and yields a much smaller interaction (-0.072) that is not significant (exact permutation P = 0.16). Note that this detected-gene analysis is a *subset* sensitivity, not a multiplicity correction; it also shows M1 itself shifting downward in the detected-gene subset (P = 0.046, §3.6), so the apparent full-module selectivity is better described as dilution-limited than as a confirmed biological dissociation. The channel-class imbalance is instead suggested at the gene level on a nominal, uncorrected basis (TRPV1 survives within-E1 correction, EZR and PXN survive within-M2 correction descriptively, KCNS1/SCN9A/ASIC1/2 are nominally down, and no M1 gene reaches P < 0.05 [PIEZO2 P = 0.18]) but remains hypothesis-generating pending family-corrected confirmation. We disclose that the contrast-of-contrasts interaction test was specified post hoc (not in the original frozen analysis plan) and is reported as exploratory; because two routes were tested, the nominal interaction P should be read with that multiplicity in mind (the 2-route Bonferroni-adjusted exosome interaction P = 0.0034 remains significant, but the detected-gene subset analysis above renders the selectivity non-significant). The VZV picture remains weak and inconsistent: direct infection drove M1 downward (-0.050; Welch P = 0.047) while E1 was unchanged (+0.0005; P = 0.996), and individual E1 genes were nominally down under HZ exosomes (TRPV1 P < 0.001, SCN9A P = 0.018, ASIC1/2 P ≤ 0.042) whereas no M1 gene reached significance (PIEZO2 P = 0.18), consistent with a gene-level, route-dependent E1 signal rather than a module-level selective suppression. The route dependence is one of magnitude rather than direction: TRPV1 mRNA also fell under direct VZV infection (-0.149 log2CPM, Welch P = 0.008, within-E1 q = 0.144), roughly a quarter of the HZ-exosome effect (-0.567); PIEZO2 likewise fell modestly under direct infection (-0.144, P = 0.021).

### 3.5 Exploratory PHN-exosome contrast: non-specific and confounded

PHN-serum exosomes produced larger and broader shifts than HZ exosomes: M1 (-0.119; P = 0.009; q = 0.024), E1 (-0.197; P = 0.005; q = 0.020), M2 (-0.178; P = 0.030), but also the neutral control N1 (-0.198; P = 0.039), together with 568 genes up- and 125 down-regulated at |log2FC| > 0.5 (versus 129/174 under HZ exosomes) and a large ribosomal induction (N3 +0.577; P = 0.001; q = 0.010). Because a "specific" mechanistic signal cannot be distinguished from a neutral control module shifting in the same direction, and because the PHN donor group is older and almost exclusively female,^6^ we report this contrast as non-specific and confounded, and it plays no confirmatory role in our conclusions.

Donor-level age and sex are not present in the deposited GEO metadata for these samples (which report only cell line and treatment), so the confounding cannot be quantified from public data; it is documented in the original publication^6^ and is the reason this contrast is treated as exploratory rather than confirmatory. Gene-level P values within this contrast are reported without any claim of family-wise control, and no clinical inference about PHN as a disease of patients is drawn from it.

### 3.6 Neutral controls, calibration, and reporting discipline

Three neutral-control observations qualify the interpretation. First, N2 (olfactory receptors) reached nominal significance under HZ exosomes (-0.022; P = 0.006; q = 0.023) despite a magnitude near the detection floor: the signal rests on a single low-baseline gene (OR5A1, 0.89 CPM, 32% detection). This is precisely why the plan mandates reporting effect size alongside every P value; N2 is interpreted as a calibration benchmark, not a biological finding. Second, N3 (ribosomal proteins) was induced by HZ exosomes (+0.391; P = 0.002; q = 0.014) and more strongly by PHN exosomes (+0.577; P = 0.001); it behaves as an exposure (positive) control reflecting a genuine translational-program response to exosome exposure, not as a neutral baseline. Under the eight-module Benjamini–Hochberg family for the primary contrast, the full ordering of q values was N3 0.014, N2 0.023, E1 0.034, M2 0.136, M1 0.181, N1 0.201 and M3/M4 0.462: the two neutral controls outrank the E1 secondary endpoint and are interpreted as an exposure response and a calibration benchmark, respectively, not as biological findings. Third, N1 overlaps E1 by two genes (KCNQ2, KCNQ3) that move with E1 under PHN exosomes; the N1 "control" signal under PHN therefore shares machinery with the E1 signal and is disclosed as such. Because these two genes belong to both modules, their within-module q values are family-specific and are reported separately rather than as a single number: under the primary contrast, KCNQ3 has q = 0.366 within E1 and q = 0.500 within N1, and KCNQ2 has q = 0.497 within E1 and q = 0.564 within N1; neither is significant in either family. Global-shift diagnostics confirmed no genome-wide drift confounded the module scores: per-sample mean log2CPM values were near-identical across all 19 exosome samples (range 1.55 to 1.61); this diagnostic is a blunt instrument dominated by low-count genes, and the normalization sensitivity analysis (§2.3) is the substantive answer to the drift question. The CCI did not differ between groups at the full gene set under any contrast (all permutation P >= 0.82: HZ exosomes +82.7, P = 0.83; PHN exosomes -74.3, P = 0.85; direct infection -19.8, P = 0.97; Figure 2d). This null is not a construction artifact: the 18 E1 genes moved a mean of 596 within-sample rank positions downward between control and HZ exosomes (GRIA1 2,868; SCN10A 1,396; TRPV1 1,042), and the CCI is sensitive to displacements of this size: a uniform -0.22 log2CPM shift imposed on the E1 genes of the HZ group would produce a CCI difference of about +2,470 (P = 0.0006). The observed null instead reflects cancellation by the M1 arm, whose displacement is dominated by below-detection TRPA1 (mean CPM 0.033 versus 0.007; -5,451 rank positions); restricted to detected genes (5 M1, 13 E1), the CCI becomes +215.6 (permutation P = 0.046). The CCI is therefore reported as a descriptive rank-based observation rather than as a negative control, and its direction under the detected-gene restriction is consistent with an absolute component to the E1 suppression; no claim of channel-class specificity is drawn from it. A sensitivity analysis restricting module scores to detected genes (CPM > 1) left the conclusions unchanged for E1 but narrowed the M1 null: under HZ exosomes, detected-gene M1 flipped to -0.171 (P = 0.046), indicating that the "spared" M1 interpretation is resolution-limited and should be read alongside the gene-level PIEZO2 null (P = 0.18).

### 3.7 Neuronal integrity: the E1 shift is not attributable to overt injury

To distinguish a specific channel effect from a non-specific neuronal reaction or cytotoxicity, we examined the injury marker ATF3 and a pan-neuronal panel (post hoc). ATF3 was unchanged under HZ exosomes (+0.0002 log2CPM, P = 0.998), so the E1 suppression cannot be attributed to overt neuronal injury or cell loss. However, five pan-neuronal markers moved modestly in the same direction (NEFH -0.221, P = 0.006; TUBB3 -0.183, P = 0.012; NTRK1 -0.202, P = 0.031; PRPH -0.128, P = 0.048; NEFL -0.111, P = 0.053), so a mild, non-specific neuronal response accompanying the exosome exposure cannot be excluded; no marker survived Benjamini-Hochberg correction across the ten-gene panel. This pattern bounds the interpretation in both directions: it argues against cytotoxicity as the explanation for the E1 shift, while cautioning that the shift is not demonstrably channel-specific at the level of the whole transcriptome.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 A route-dependent, gene-level channel signal: hypothesis-generating, not a proven allodynia substrate

The central empirical contribution of this reanalysis is a gene-level channel-class imbalance: acute-HZ serum exosomes suppress the electrochemical nociceptor apparatus (led by TRPV1, with nominal down-regulation of KCNS1, Nav1.7/SCN9A and ASIC1/2) more than the mechanically gated one (E1 genes nominally down; only TRPV1 survives within-E1 correction; no M1 gene significant), while the ECM-Hippo stiffness program shows no detectable transcriptional response within a bounded null. The full-module interaction that initially suggested selective E1 suppression (P = 0.0017) did not survive a detected-gene subset analysis (P = 0.16), so we do not present it as a confirmed dissociation; the formal route-by-program difference-in-differences (-0.193 log2CPM; exact permutation P = 0.0011) supports route dependence at the level of perturbation route, not a confirmed cross-program dissociation. We deliberately do not present this as a transcriptomic "substrate for mechanical allodynia." The established mechanotransduction literature points the opposite way: allodynia is predominantly a gain of mechanosensory function, and Piezo2 is a mechanically activated cation channel expressed by touch receptors and by a large fraction of DRG neurons,^18-20^ whose gain-of-function sensitizes nociceptors and increases mechanical pain sensitivity;^21^ indeed, that same work implies allodynia requires intact Piezo2 function. Because we invoke mRNA-function decoupling to reconcile the E1 direction (below), the same logic must be applied here: PIEZO2 transcript was unchanged, but Piezo2 function is regulated post-translationally, most directly by relief of its voltage-block, which is sufficient to sensitize cutaneous nociceptors and drive ongoing activity without any change in PIEZO2 expression.^21^ Our data therefore show no transcriptional up-regulation of the mechanogated program in this model. Piezo2-dependent mechanical sensitization remains possible, because Piezo2 function is regulated post-translationally (most directly by relief of its voltage block), so the M1 null should not be read as evidence against a mechanical axis in PHN. Piezo2 is required for mechanical pain in mice^22^ and human loss-of-function causes selective loss of discriminative touch and proprioception,^23^ and SCN9A loss-of-function causes congenital insensitivity to pain.^24^

The directionality must be addressed explicitly. We observe reduced steady-state transcript of pro-nociceptive channels (TRPV1, Nav1.7, ASICs), yet PHN mechanical allodynia is a state of increased mechanosensitivity. Three non-mutually-exclusive reconciliations are biologically grounded. (i) mRNA–function decoupling: PHN allodynia is driven predominantly by post-translational sensitization of these channels (phosphorylation, trafficking, and inflammatory mediators such as NGF, PGE2 and bradykinin), so reduced steady-state transcript is compatible with functional hyperexcitability provided that post-translational sensitization outweighs an approximately 1.5-fold reduction in transcript; we cannot test this balance without protein-level or electrophysiological data, and the alternative reading, that the observed down-regulation is genuinely counter-regulatory or homeostatic, remains open. The same decoupling applies to the M3 program, which is also largely post-translationally regulated. (ii) Exosome as host-signal readout: patient serum exosomes are a readout of the systemic milieu during acute HZ; the observed down-regulation may reflect a host-derived homeostatic or anti-nociceptive signal rather than a neuronal-autonomous pro-allodynia program, with allodynia arising where this containment fails. (iii) Model limitation: an embryonic clonal line cannot recapture in-vivo circuit-level hyperexcitability. We therefore do not present the shift as a direct allodynia mechanism; its relevance is hypothesis-generating and model-bound. Clinically, this raises a testable question: whether the channel-suppressive response of sensory neurons to acute-HZ serum exosomes is transient or persistent, and whether its persistence distinguishes patients whose pain resolves from those who progress to PHN. A well-posed test would enroll consecutive patients with acute herpes zoster at rash onset, collect serum before antiviral treatment where feasible, expose the same HD10.6 assay to blinded exosome aliquots at fixed intervals (for example baseline and 2, 4, 8 and 12 weeks), pre-specify PHN at 90 days as the endpoint with progression-free patients as the comparator group, and adjust for age, sex and analgesic exposure. The assay's reproducibility, its dependence on exosome dose, and the non-specific PHN-exosome behavior observed here would all need to be resolved before such a study could be powered; the present data only motivate it. (The source study's report of up-regulated substance P is not reproduced here: TAC1 was +0.22 log2CPM, P = 0.38, under HZ exosomes.)

### 4.2 Route dependence and its implications

The interaction test shows the route of perturbation matters, but asymmetrically: exosomes from acute zoster produce a clear E1 suppression at the gene level (a nominal signal led by TRPV1), whereas direct VZV infection does not produce a statistically supported selective M1 effect. The formal route-by-program difference-in-differences (-0.193 log2CPM; exact permutation P = 0.0011) supports this asymmetry statistically. This argues against treating "VZV effects on sensory neurons" as a single biological category: intracellular viral replication and systemic extracellular-vesicle signaling are not interchangeable models, and conclusions from one cannot be exported to the other. It also identifies exosomes as the plausible carrier of the systemic-to-neuronal signal that follows acute zoster, aligning with the literature on extracellular-vesicle-mediated pain signaling, in which DRG-neuron- and macrophage-derived EVs bidirectionally regulate nociceptive signaling and neuropathic mechanical hypersensitivity.^25,26^ We note the E1≫M1 selectivity does not survive detection-floor correction (§3.4), so the route difference is best stated as "exosomes yield a clear E1 signal; direct infection does not yield a selective M1 signal" rather than a confirmed cross-program dissociation.

### 4.3 The inert stiffness program redirects the mechanical search

The most informative result for the mechanotransduction field may be what did not move. The ECM-integrin-Hippo program, the transcriptional core of cellular mechanotransduction, showed no detectable transcriptional response under any perturbation route, within a bounded null (MDE 0.18 log2CPM); "no detectable response" is the claim, not "inert by mechanism". Three explanations deserve attention. (i) The program may not be neuron-intrinsic: in DRG, ECM deposition, stiffness sensing and YAP/TAZ dynamics are dominated by satellite glia, fibroblasts and endothelial cells rather than neurons, so bulk neuronal cultures are the wrong compartment to observe it. (ii) The 36-hour window captures early channel-level events, whereas ECM remodeling requires longer time constants. (iii) mRNA may be uncoupled from mechanotransduction, which is largely regulated post-translationally. Radial extracorporeal shockwave therapy (rESWT), which this section invokes only as contextual background, illustrates the compartmental problem at the experimental level: in single-cell data from a rat bone-callus model, ESWT released extracellular vesicles through an ATP/P2X7R/p38MAPK axis acting on mesenchymal rather than neuronal cells,^27^ and none of those studies tested a YAP/TAZ or neuronal-channel mechanism in PHN. Whichever applies, the practical redirection is the same: the mechanical axis in PHN should be pursued in non-neuronal compartments (DRG satellite glia, skin, and serum) and in spatially resolved tissue data. This reframing is relevant only as context for mechanotherapy: the machinery engaged by ESWT (integrin-FAK-p38MAPK; Piezo1–FAK signaling) resides in those non-neuronal compartments,^11,12^ and ESWT has shown within-group benefit in herpetic neuralgia in small uncontrolled or single-blind studies^7,8^ although the largest randomized trial reported pain reduction without a significant time-by-group interaction.^9^ We do not assert any YAP/TAZ- or neuronal-channel-specific ESWT mechanism: the cited works do not test such a mechanism, and the present data show the neuronal mechanogated and stiffness programs to be inert, so the ESWT literature is contextual background for future non-neuronal studies, not evidence supporting the present findings.

### 4.4 Limitations

Before the point-by-point constraints below, one overarching caution for clinical readers: every result in this study derives from a 36-hour exposure of an immortalized embryonic dorsal-root-ganglion clonal line (HD10.6) to patient-derived serum exosomes or VZV. No finding here describes chronic PHN biology in living patients, and the PHN-exosome donor group, the clinically most relevant contrast, is confounded by age and sex; conclusions about PHN as a disease of patients are out of scope.

This study is a secondary analysis with the following constraints, each stated at design stage. (i) The model is an immortalized embryonic DRG clonal line (HD10.6), not iPSC-derived neurons and not DRG tissue; its baseline differs from adult DRG: PIEZO2 is in fact widely expressed across adult human DRG neuronal classes, so PIEZO2-high is not itself the deviation, whereas TRPV4/STOML3 absence and single-clone derivation are,^14^ and all findings are model-bound. Because a single clone cannot represent the peptidergic/non-peptidergic/NF subtypes of adult DRG, the term "human sensory neurons" in this paper should be read as "this embryonic clonal line." (ii) The exosome exposure is acute (36 hours); conclusions are restricted to early molecular events and cannot be extrapolated to chronic PHN in vivo. (iii) Bulk RNA-sequencing reports neither cell-composition changes nor post-translational mechanotransduction. (iv) PHN-exosome donors are older and almost exclusively female, and the exploratory PHN contrast accordingly behaved non-specifically. (v) Two panel genes (CYR61, CTGF) were absent from the processed matrix, and TRPV4/STOML3/MMP9 were below detection, bounding the scope of M1/M3 inference (the CCN1/CCN2 alias sensitivity, §3.2, leaves the M3 null unchanged); the absence of TRPV4 and STOML3, the most mechanosensitive members of M1, means the mechanogated module was not fully tested for mechanical-gating function; moreover P2RX3, a purinergic rather than mechanically gated receptor, dominates M1, so the "mechanogated" inference rests largely on PIEZO2, whose gene-level response was a resolution-limited null; SCN10A/Nav1.8 lay below the detection floor in the exosome experiment, so its down-regulation is descriptive only. (vi) The direct-infection M1 effect is borderline (permutation P = 0.05) and the reciprocal dissociation is unsupported by the interaction test (P = 0.19); the route-dependence claim rests on the module-level E1 suppression under exosomes, while its E1-selective aspect is nominal and gene-level (see xi), and requires confirmation in independent models. (vii) The analysis is hypothesis-generating throughout: no causal, mechanistic, or therapeutic claim is made. (viii) Exosome identity and cargo were not verified in this reanalysis; the neuronal response could reflect co-isolated soluble factors (cytokines, lipoproteins, free nucleic acids, ATP) rather than vesicular cargo, since standard serum-exosome preparations co-isolate such factors and no depletion/vesicle-disruption control was performed. (ix) SCN10A/Nav1.8 baseline differed markedly between the two GSE342782 experiments, mean CPM 7.23 in DirectInf versus 0.55 in SeraExo, a ~13-fold difference, constraining cross-experiment comparability of the E1 module; PIEZO2 showed a smaller but non-trivial cross-experiment difference (245.2 versus 164.1 CPM, ~1.5-fold), so M1 comparisons across the two experiments are likewise descriptive. (x) The source study's reported up-regulation of substance P was not reproduced here (TAC1 +0.22 log2CPM, P = 0.38, under HZ exosomes); the present data show only the channel-downregulation pattern.

Three further constraints are noted. (xi) The E1 signal is not separable from the neutral N1 control at the module level: N1 itself shifted by -0.156 log2CPM under HZ exosomes (permutation P = 0.151) versus E1 -0.220 (P = 0.013), and their difference gives E1 - N1 = -0.064 (P = 0.178). Any claim of channel-class selectivity is therefore nominal and gene-level, not a demonstrated module-level dissociation. (xii) Substrate stiffness, the primary determinant of cellular mechanotransduction, was neither manipulated nor measured: the neurons were cultured on standard plastic, so the absence of an ECM-Hippo response may reflect an unphysiological mechanical set-point rather than neuronal insensitivity. (xiii) The preparation is neurons alone; satellite glia, immune cells and spinal circuitry, the compartments in which PHN allodynia is generated and maintained, are absent, and the exosome dose was not independently quantified or dose-ranged, so no dose-response inference is possible. (xiv) Module scores are unweighted means of log2(1+CPM) across panel genes; alternative scorings (rank-based, gene-weighted, or first principal component) were not tested, so the module-level P values are conditional on this scoring choice, one reason the gene-level results are reported alongside every module-level claim. (xv) All findings are transcriptional: no protein abundance, membrane localization, or electrophysiological measurement was performed, so channel function, the quantity relevant to allodynia, is not directly assessed. (xvi) Whether the exosome-treated replicates represent independent donor sera or include technical replicates cannot be determined from the public metadata; the effective sample size, and hence the minimum detectable effect, is conditional on their independence.

## 5. Conclusions

In a prospectively specified reanalysis of the only available human sensory-neuron resource covering VZV infection and patient-derived serum exosomes, acute-HZ serum exosomes produced a route-dependent down-regulation of the electrogenic nociceptor program that, at the gene level and on a nominal basis, was not matched by the mechanically gated one (TRPV1 significant after within-E1 correction; KCNS1, SCN9A, ASIC1/2 nominally down; no M1 gene significant; M1 itself resolution-limited, flipping to significant when detected genes only are analyzed, P = 0.046), with route dependence formally supported by a route-by-program difference-in-differences (-0.193 log2CPM; exact permutation P = 0.0011), while the ECM-Hippo stiffness program showed no detectable transcriptional response within a bounded null (minimum detectable effect 0.18 log2CPM), a negative result that redirects the mechanical-axis search toward non-neuronal compartments. The full-module interaction initially suggestive of selective E1 suppression (P = 0.0017) did not survive a detected-gene subset analysis (P = 0.16), so the channel-class imbalance is reported as gene-level and hypothesis-generating. The exploratory PHN-exosome contrast was non-specific and confounded by donor age and sex, and is reported as such. The relevance of these shifts to mechanical allodynia in PHN is hypothesis-generating and model-bound: all conclusions derive from a 36-h exposure of an immortalized embryonic dorsal-root-ganglion line and cannot be extrapolated to chronic PHN in living patients, but they frame a testable clinical question, whether the HZ-exosome channel-suppressive signature predicts progression to PHN.
