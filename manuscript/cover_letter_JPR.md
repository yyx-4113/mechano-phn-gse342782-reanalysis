# Cover Letter — Journal of Pain Research

**Manuscript type:** Original Research
**Title:** Route-dependent transcriptional effects of varicella-zoster virus and patient serum exosomes on ion-channel programs in human sensory neurons: a prospectively specified reanalysis of GSE342782

---

Dear Editor,

Please consider our original research article, *“Route-dependent transcriptional effects of varicella-zoster virus and patient serum exosomes on ion-channel programs in human sensory neurons: a prospectively specified reanalysis of GSE342782,”* for publication in *Journal of Pain Research*.

Postherpetic neuralgia (PHN) is clinically defined by mechanical allodynia, yet the mechanotransduction machinery that underlies this cardinal symptom has never been systematically examined in the genomic literature of the disease. Our prospectively specified reanalysis of GSE342782 — the only available human sensory-neuron resource combining direct varicella-zoster virus (VZV) infection with patient-derived serum exosomes from healthy controls, acute herpes zoster, and PHN — addresses this gap directly. The work sits within the journal's scope on neuropathic-pain mechanisms and the cellular basis of sensory dysfunction.

We report a route-dependent, gene-level channel signal led by TRPV1. Acute-HZ serum exosomes suppressed the electrochemical nociceptor channel program (E1 module P = 0.013, q = 0.034); at the gene level TRPV1 was down-regulated (P = 0.0009) and remained significant after Benjamini–Hochberg correction within the E1 module (q = 0.017), whereas KCNS1, Nav1.7/SCN9A and ASIC1/2 were nominally down but did not survive within-E1 correction (q ≈ 0.07–0.13), and no mechanogated M1 gene was significant (PIEZO2 P = 0.18). The ECM-integrin-Hippo stiffness program was transcriptionally inert within a bounded null (minimum detectable effect at 80% power = 0.18 log2CPM; 95% CI -0.18 to +0.08). A full-module contrast-of-contrasts interaction initially suggested selective E1 suppression (P = 0.0017), but this did not survive a detected-gene subset analysis (P = 0.16) and is reported as exploratory/post hoc; the channel-class imbalance is therefore reported as gene-level and nominal, read as hypothesis-generating. We thus present the finding as model-bound, not as an established allodynia mechanism: allodynia in the literature is predominantly a gain of mechanosensory function (Piezo2 gain-of-function sensitizes nociceptors; SCN9A loss-of-function causes insensitivity to pain), so reduced electrochemical-channel expression in this neuronal model is most parsimoniously a shift in signaling mode — compatible with functional hyperexcitability via post-translational sensitization — requiring in vivo validation.

Methodologically, the analysis plan was specified prospectively before any data were examined, with a frozen four-module mechano-transduction panel, a single pre-specified primary endpoint, three neutral-control modules, and exact label-permutation inference enumerating every C(n1+n2, n1) partition (no Monte-Carlo approximation, no 1/B floor). A full reproducibility package with a SHA-256 MANIFEST regenerates every reported number from the raw GEO files. We report a bounded null at the primary endpoint alongside the pre-specified secondary positive signal, with effect sizes throughout, so that negative and positive findings are equally transparent.

This study is a secondary analysis of publicly available, de-identified GEO data (GSE342782) derived from the HD10.6 line — a tetracycline-regulatable v-myc immortalized embryonic dorsal root ganglion clonal line, not an iPSC-derived model and not DRG tissue. No new human-subject research, patient recruitment, or animal work was conducted; no additional ethics approval or informed consent was required beyond that of the original data deposition.

The authors declare no competing interests and no funding. The manuscript is original, has not been published elsewhere, and is not under consideration by another journal. Suggested reviewers can be provided on request.
